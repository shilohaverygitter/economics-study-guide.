1. Interest Rates & The Bank of Canada

Why does the Bank of Canada raise interest rates?

  • To control high inflation.
  • Higher interest rates make borrowing money more expensive.
  • People and businesses borrow and spend less.
  • Lower demand can help slow down price increases.

How does this affect households?

  • Mortgage payments and loan payments can increase.
  • Credit card debt becomes more expensive.
  • People may save more because savings accounts can earn more interest.
  • Families may spend less on non-essential items.
Remember: Higher interest rates = less borrowing and spending = lower demand = less inflation.

2. Inflation & Cost of Living

Inflation is the general increase in prices over time.

Purchasing Power

Purchasing power is how much you can buy with your money.

Example:

  • Prices increase by 6%.
  • Wages increase by only 2%.
  • Your purchasing power decreases because your income isn't keeping up with prices.

Why are fixed incomes affected?

  • People receiving the same income have less money to spend as prices rise.
  • They cannot easily increase their income to keep up with inflation.
  • They may struggle to afford necessities like groceries and housing.

3. Interest Rates & Businesses

When interest rates increase, businesses have to pay more to borrow money.

Example: Construction company

  • A $2 million loan becomes more expensive.
  • The company might delay or cancel the apartment project.
  • It may decide the project is no longer profitable.

Possible consequences for the economy

  • Fewer construction jobs are created.
  • Less housing gets built.
  • Economic growth may slow down.
  • Businesses may invest less money.

4. Consumer Price Index (CPI)

CPI measures the average change in the prices of a basket of common goods and services that households buy.

How does the government use CPI?

  • Understand how the cost of living is changing.
  • Make decisions about interest rates and inflation.
  • Adjust certain government benefits and pensions.
  • Help determine whether people need additional financial support.
  • Develop economic policies to support Canadians.
Important: CPI helps the government understand how inflation affects everyday Canadians.

5. Unemployment & Factory Closures

If a major factory closes and 2,000 people lose their jobs, the surrounding community can experience several economic problems.

  1. Lower consumer spending: Unemployed workers have less money to spend at local businesses.
  2. Business closures: Local restaurants and stores may lose customers and revenue.
  3. Lower government revenue: The government may collect less income and sales tax while spending more on employment support.

6. Trade Surplus vs. Trade Deficit

Trade Surplus: A country exports more goods and services than it imports.
Trade Deficit: A country imports more goods and services than it exports.

Easy way to remember:

  • Surplus = selling more than buying.
  • Deficit = buying more than selling.

7. GDP & Standard of Living

GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is the total value of goods and services produced within a country over a period of time.

Why doesn't a higher GDP always mean a better standard of living?

  • A country with a larger population might have a higher total GDP.
  • GDP doesn't show how equally wealth is distributed.
  • It doesn't directly measure quality of life.

Other factors to consider

  • GDP per capita: GDP divided by the population. Shows average economic output per person.
  • Income inequality: Shows how wealth is distributed among citizens.
  • Cost of living: How expensive basic necessities are.
  • Healthcare and education: Access to important services.
Remember: GDP per capita is generally more useful than total GDP when comparing countries' economic output per person.

8. Oil Prices & The Canadian Economy

Canada is a major oil-producing country. When oil prices increase, there are both positive and negative effects.

a) Consumers & Cost of Living

  • Gasoline and transportation become more expensive.
  • Shipping costs increase, which can raise the prices of other goods.

b) Oil Companies & Workers

  • Oil companies can earn more revenue and potentially higher profits.
  • They may invest more and hire additional workers.

c) Inflation

  • Higher fuel and transportation costs can increase the prices of goods and services.
  • This can increase Canada's inflation rate.

d) Balance of Trade

  • Canada earns more from exporting oil if the price increases and export volumes remain similar.
  • This can improve Canada's trade balance.

e) Canadian Dollar

  • Higher oil prices can increase demand for Canadian dollars.
  • This can cause the Canadian dollar to appreciate (increase in value).
Remember: Higher oil prices can benefit Canadian oil producers but make life more expensive for Canadian consumers.

9. Economic Challenges in Canada

Imagine Canada is experiencing:

  • Inflation of 7%.
  • Unemployment of 8%.
  • GDP declining for two consecutive quarters.
  • Consumer spending decreasing.
  • Businesses delaying investments.

Major economic problems

  • High inflation: Prices are rising quickly.
  • High unemployment: More people are without jobs.
  • Economic recession: GDP declining for two consecutive quarters is a commonly used definition of a technical recession.

Why might raising interest rates make things worse?

  • Borrowing becomes more expensive.
  • Consumers spend less.
  • Businesses invest less.
  • Unemployment could increase further.
  • Economic growth could slow even more.

What could the Bank of Canada do?

It could consider lowering interest rates to encourage borrowing, spending and business investment.

This could help stimulate economic growth and reduce unemployment, although it could also make inflation worse.

Inflation vs. Unemployment

There isn't one automatically correct answer about which is the bigger concern. You need to defend your opinion.

Argument for inflation: High inflation reduces purchasing power and makes necessities less affordable for everyone.
Argument for unemployment: People without jobs lose their income, making it difficult to afford necessities and support their families.

10. Three Factors That Impact Currency Value

Three major economic factors commonly used to explain changes in a country's currency are:

  1. Interest rates: Higher interest rates can attract foreign investors, increasing demand for the currency.
  2. Inflation: Lower inflation can help maintain a currency's purchasing power and value.
  3. Economic performance: A strong economy can attract foreign investment and increase demand for its currency.
Also remember: Supply and demand for a currency in the foreign exchange market ultimately determine its exchange rate. Check that these are the three factors your teacher used in class.

Important Economic Vocabulary

Inflation: A general increase in prices over time.
Interest Rate: The cost of borrowing money or the return earned on savings.
Bank of Canada: Canada's central bank, responsible for monetary policy and maintaining price stability.
CPI: Consumer Price Index. Measures changes in the prices of a basket of consumer goods and services.
Purchasing Power: How much goods and services your money can buy.
GDP: Gross Domestic Product. The total value of goods and services produced in a country.
GDP Per Capita: GDP divided by the population.
Unemployment: People who are without work, available for work and actively looking for a job.
Recession: A significant decline in economic activity. Two consecutive quarters of declining GDP is a common technical definition.
Trade Surplus: Exports are greater than imports.
Trade Deficit: Imports are greater than exports.
Balance of Trade: The difference between the value of a country's exports and imports of goods.
Appreciation: When a currency increases in value compared to another currency.
Depreciation: When a currency decreases in value compared to another currency.
Monetary Policy: Actions taken by the Bank of Canada to influence interest rates and economic conditions.
Standard of Living: The overall quality of life and access to goods, services and necessities.
Cost of Living: The amount of money needed to afford basic expenses.
Consumer Spending: Money households spend on goods and services.
Investment: Money businesses spend to expand or improve operations.

Short Answer Practice

Try answering each question yourself before opening the suggested answer.

1. Why does the Bank of Canada raise interest rates when inflation is high?

Raising interest rates makes borrowing more expensive. This encourages people and businesses to spend and borrow less, reducing demand and helping slow inflation.

2. How does a 6% increase in prices and a 2% wage increase affect purchasing power?

Purchasing power decreases because prices increased faster than wages. The family can afford less with its income.

3. How do higher interest rates affect construction companies?

Higher interest rates increase the cost of borrowing. The company may delay or cancel its project. This could lead to fewer jobs and less housing being built.

4. How does CPI help the government?

CPI helps the government track inflation and changes in the cost of living. It can use this information to adjust benefits, pensions and economic policies to support citizens.

5. Give three consequences of a major factory closing.

Unemployment increases, local businesses lose customers and revenue, and government tax revenue may decrease while spending on employment support increases.

6. What is the difference between a trade surplus and deficit?

A trade surplus occurs when exports are greater than imports. A trade deficit occurs when imports are greater than exports.

7. Why doesn't a higher GDP always mean a higher standard of living?

A country may have a larger population, meaning its GDP is spread across more people. You should also consider GDP per capita, income inequality and cost of living.

8. Explain five effects of rising oil prices.

Consumers pay more for gas and transportation. Oil companies may earn more. Inflation may increase due to higher production and shipping costs. Canada's trade balance may improve if oil export prices increase. The Canadian dollar may appreciate because of increased demand.

9. Why might increasing interest rates make Canada's economic situation worse?

Higher rates make borrowing more expensive, reducing consumer spending and business investment. This could increase unemployment and slow economic growth even further.

10. What are three factors that affect currency value?

Interest rates, inflation and economic performance. Supply and demand in foreign exchange markets also determine exchange rates.
